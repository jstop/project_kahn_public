# Wes Conversation: Preparation Brief

**Prepared for:** Josh (Director of Communications, Osmio)
**Subject:** Attribution, authority, and strategic alignment for the Voluntary Polity Stack and Agent Commons
**Date:** March 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document prepares you for a conversation with Wes Kussmaul to resolve three questions that are blocking all public-facing work:

1. **Sanctioning:** Does Wes see the VPS and Agent Commons framing as sanctioned Osmio strategy, or as your independent project built on Osmio infrastructure?
2. **Attribution:** How does he want provenance and attribution handled in public materials?
3. **Go-to-market:** Is he comfortable with the Agent Commons as a go-to-market angle for Osmio's trust infrastructure?

| If Osmio-sanctioned | If independent project |
|---|---|

| Materials say "Osmio presents..." | Materials say "Built on Osmio infrastructure" |

Neither outcome is bad. But the hybrid — where it's ambiguous — is the worst case. That's what you're resolving.

## 4. How to Frame the Conversation

### Opening frame (say something like this)

> "Wes, I want to talk about attribution, authority, and where the work I've been doing fits relative to Osmio. I've built something I'm excited about — the Voluntary Polity Stack architecture and the Agent Commons application — and it's grounded in your decades of work. Before I take any of it public, I want to make sure we're aligned on three things: whether this is Osmio strategy or my independent project, how you want credit and provenance handled, and whether the Agent Commons angle is a direction you're comfortable with for Osmio's infrastructure. I'd rather have this conversation now than discover a misalignment after things are public."

### Why this framing works
- **Leads with respect:** You're asking permission, not announcing decisions
- **Names the dependency:** "Before I take any of it public" — signals you've deliberately waited
- **Scopes the conversation:** Three specific questions, not an open-ended discussion
- **Creates space for any answer:** You're not presupposing the outcome

## 5. The Three Questions — How to Ask Them

### Question 1: Sanctioning

**Ask:** "Do you see the VPS and Agent Commons as part of Osmio's strategic roadmap — something we're doing together as Osmio — or as my independent project that happens to build on Osmio infrastructure?"

**Listen for:**
- "This is exactly what Osmio should be doing" → He wants it inside Osmio
- "That's your project, I think it's great" → He's blessing independence
- "I hadn't thought of it that way" → He needs time; don't push for an answer in the moment
- "I have concerns about..." → There's a specific issue; dig into it

**If he's ambiguous:** "I want to be concrete — when I publish this skill on OpenClaw and pitch it to developers, should it come from Osmio's GitHub org, or from my personal account with 'Built on Osmio' in the attribution? That distinction drives a lot of downstream decisions."

**What you should be ready to accept:** Either answer works for you. If it's Osmio, you have institutional backing. If it's independent, you have full creative control. Say this out loud so he knows there's no wrong answer.

### Question 2: Attribution

**Ask:** "How do you want provenance handled? I've been careful to distinguish between your foundational concepts — Accountable Anonymity, PKIDR, the municipal CA model — and the things I've built on top of them: the five-layer Stack architecture, the Epistemic Commitment Protocol, the Agent Commons application. Does that distinction feel right to you, or would you frame the provenance differently?"

**Listen for:**
- Specific corrections ("I wouldn't say X is mine, that's really...") — take notes, incorporate
- Generosity ("You've done the real work of making it concrete") — accept graciously but maintain the distinction
- Concern ("I worry that the Stack framing might overshadow...") — this is the core anxiety; address it directly

**If he pushes back on the framing:** "Help me understand how you'd describe the relationship. I want to get this right in every public document."

**Key principle:** You can always give more credit. You can never claw it back without awkwardness. Err toward generous attribution of his foundational work.

### Question 3: Go-to-market

**Ask:** "The Agent Commons application — using Osmio's trust infrastructure to solve the cross-boundary coordination problem in AI agent ecosystems — is that a direction you're comfortable with for Osmio? It would position Osmio's PKI as critical infrastructure for AI, not just human identity."

**Listen for:**
- Enthusiasm → He sees the market opportunity
- Caution ("I need to understand the technical implications") → He wants to review specifics before endorsing
- Concern about dilution ("I don't want Osmio to become an AI company") → He's protective of Osmio's core mission
- Strategic disagreement ("I think the better market is...") → He has a different vision for Osmio's growth

**If he's cautious:** "Would you be comfortable with a limited pilot — one skill, one community, see what the response is — before we commit to this as a direction?"

## 7. Pitfalls to Avoid

**Don't minimize his contribution.** Even if you believe the Stack architecture is the novel contribution, the concepts it organizes are his life's work. If he feels his work is being repackaged without adequate credit, the conversation will go sideways regardless of the structural question.

**Don't ask for permission you don't need.** If you've built the VPS architecture and the Agent Commons thesis using publicly available concepts and open infrastructure, you have the right to publish it. You're having this conversation out of respect and strategic alignment, not legal necessity. Don't frame it as if he has veto power unless you genuinely want to give him veto power.

**Don't conflate the three questions.** Attribution and sanctioning are separate. He might want full credit (attribution) but not want to own the project (sanctioning). Or he might want it under Osmio (sanctioning) but be relaxed about how credit is described (attribution). Keep the questions distinct.

**Don't fill silence.** If he pauses to think, let him think. The worst thing you can do is interpret silence as resistance and start negotiating against yourself.

**Don't pitch the Agent Commons.** He's not a prospect. He's a colleague and the founder of the infrastructure you're building on. If you start selling him on the market opportunity, it'll feel transactional. Describe what you've built and what you're planning — let him react.

**Don't leave without clarity.** Ambiguity is worse than either answer. If the conversation ends without clear answers, follow up within 24 hours with a written summary: "Here's what I heard — is this right?" Don't let it drift.

## 9. Conversation Logistics

- **Format:** In-person or video call preferred. This is not an email conversation.
- **Duration:** Block 60 minutes. You may only need 30, but don't create time pressure.
- **Preparation for Wes:** Consider sending a brief heads-up: "I'd like to talk about how the work I've been doing on the Agent Commons fits with Osmio's direction. Nothing urgent — I just want to make sure we're aligned before I start publishing." This lets him come prepared without making it feel like a confrontation.

- **What NOT to bring:** The full Agent Commons document, the VPS architecture diagram, the OpenClaw analysis, or any of the institutional materials. This is a relationship conversation, not a presentation.
